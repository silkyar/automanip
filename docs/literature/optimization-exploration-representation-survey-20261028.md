# Optimization, exploration and representation: literature review

Reviewed 2026-10-28. This is a targeted reading of 13 primary papers, alongside the separate [model/replay](model-and-replay-survey-20261028.md) and [credit/plasticity](credit-memory-plasticity-survey-20261028.md) reviews. No new training, simulator probes or algorithm implementation was performed for this review.

The question is what could reduce the interactions needed to learn a manipulation task, and what evidence would distinguish the mechanisms. Neither a better terminal score nor a biological analogy establishes sample efficiency. Reading depth is stated below; none of these papers was reproduced here.

## Optimization and trustworthy comparisons

| Paper and reading depth | Mechanism and evidence | Costs, limits and implication for AutoManip |
|---|---|---|
| **Schulman et al., PPO (2017)** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06347). Read surrogate equations, policy-update interpolation and empirical setup. | Reuses on-policy samples through a clipped importance-ratio surrogate. Clipping removes some incentive for excessive movement; its objective is not an explicit constraint on the KL of the accepted optimizer step. | Multiple epochs cost optimization. The Gaussian latent policy and the clipped action executed by a controller are different objects. Our pre-update KL check can stop later updates without undoing an oversized accepted step. That is an implementation hypothesis, not evidence PPO cannot learn manipulation. |
| **Schulman et al., TRPO (2015)** — [paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/schulman15.pdf). Read constrained objective, practical conjugate-gradient/line-search algorithm. | Uses local policy geometry to constrain improvement. Practical line search checks a proposed move rather than assuming an optimizer learning rate enforces the trust region. | Fisher-vector products, conjugate-gradient iterations and backtracking add work. The practical average-KL approximation is not a guarantee of monotonically increasing finite-sample robot success. A directional response test is cheaper than committing to a new TRPO implementation. |
| **Kingma & Ba, Adam (2015)** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6980). Read moment updates and rescaling properties. | Coordinatewise adaptive moments make parameter movement relatively insensitive to uniform gradient scaling. With fresh moments, a first step is approximately −αg/(abs(g)+ε), coordinatewise. | Smaller gradients need not imply a smaller parameter change. Combined with Gaussian KL proportional to squared mean displacement divided by variance, this predicts sensitivity when exploration variance shrinks. This is our mathematical deduction, not a robotics result in the Adam paper; near-zero gradients, ε and established optimizer history matter. |
| **Engstrom et al., Implementation Matters (ICLR 2020)** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.12729). Read implementation ablations and ratio/KL measurements. | PPO/TRPO performance differences depend strongly on implementation details. Their measured PPO variants violate the nominal ratio interval; KL behavior depends on the optimizer and ancillary choices. | Repairs can be important without being new general learning mechanisms. Fair algorithm comparisons need matched implementation quality. Conversely, observed constraint violation alone does not prove performance loss: well-tuned PPO still performs well in their experiments. |
| **Andrychowicz et al., What Matters in On-Policy RL? (ICLR 2021)** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05990). Read study design and §§3.1–3.7, especially initialization and training setup. | More than 250,000 agents across five continuous-control tasks expose interactions among policy loss, initialization, collection size and optimization. Recomputing advantages during passes improves their results; increasing parallelism can hurt sample efficiency while helping wall time. | Their parallelism explanation partly involves shorter fragments. Our 50-step fragments stay fixed when environment count changes, so that explanation cannot be copied. Results support strong tuned baselines, not another unconstrained sweep or universal default. Their large search cost also matters. |
| **Cobbe et al., PPG (ICML 2021)** — [paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/cobbe21a/cobbe21a.pdf). Read Algorithm 1, auxiliary KL objective, §3.6. | Separates policy and value phases; distills value features into the actor while constraining policy change. Improves sample efficiency on Procgen. | Default dual-network PPG takes roughly twice PPO wall time in their implementation; their single-network variant is similar in wall time. Our actor/critic are already separate, and our failed extra-critic experiment omitted feature distillation and multi-batch auxiliary phases. It did not test or refute PPG. |

## Discovery, goal reuse and temporal abstraction

| Paper and reading depth | Mechanism and evidence | Costs, limits and implication for AutoManip |
|---|---|---|
| **Osband et al., Bootstrapped DQN (NeurIPS 2016)** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.04621). Read head sampling, chain controls, Atari results and runtime footnote. | Commits to one randomized value head through an episode. Distinguishes temporally extended exploration from independent action dithering; includes controls that resample every step. | Exponential separation is demonstrated in constructed chains, not arbitrary robotics. Atari uses ten heads; authors report under 20% wall-time overhead for their shared-network implementation. Our continuous actions and short shaped task need different implementation and evidence. A mechanism test must match action scale and ask whether useful multi-step sequences are discovered more often. |
| **Raffin et al., gSDE / Smooth Exploration (CoRL 2021)** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.05719). Read exploration equations, resampling interval, simulated/real-robot setups. | Samples a feature-dependent exploration matrix periodically, producing coherent noise with a tractable marginal distribution. Studies smoothness/performance on PyBullet and learns directly on three real robots. | This is more specific than lowering global noise. Smoothness is not itself fewer interactions or better grasp/release learning. Noise interval and feature choice are interventions; compare equally scaled independent noise and record actual action clipping. Correlation can also preserve an unhelpful grasp or prevent timely release. |
| **Andrychowicz et al., HER (NeurIPS 2017)** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.01495). Read §§3.2–3.3, goal-selection ablation and Appendix A. | Relabels achieved goals on stored transitions; the same physical experience trains multiple goal-conditioned objectives. Sparse-reward pushing, sliding and pick/place experiments provide directly relevant manipulation evidence. | Requires goals not to change dynamics and a valid recomputable goal reward. Replay and reward recomputation cost work. Their setup has eight workers and substantial training; it is not a promise of sub-million-step StackCube. Moving the physical support cube is not a virtual goal relabel: that changes dynamics. Our frozen observation and actor contracts need review before any goal-conditioned implementation. |
| **Ecoffet et al., Go-Explore (Nature 2021)** — [author paper](https://adrien.ecoffet.com/files/go-explore-nature.pdf), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919). Read archive/return/explore mechanism, robotics setup and robustification accounting. | Explicitly preserves reachable states and returns before exploring further. Separates forgetting a route from disrupting that route with exploration; demonstrates difficult Atari and sparse robotic pick/place. | Major results exploit simulator state restoration and later learn from generated trajectories. Other variants use a learned return policy, which also costs interactions. We cannot import privileged restoration or omit trajectory/robustification costs. The transferable hypothesis is reliable progress to a frontier, not free resets to grasped/stacked states. |
| **Nachum et al., HIRO (NeurIPS 2018)** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.08296). Read hierarchy, high-level goal correction and §5.2 ablations. | Learns high- and low-level policies off policy. Relabels old high-level actions to account for a changing low-level controller; evaluates long-horizon ant navigation/object interaction across ten seeds. | Hierarchy does not supply pretrained skills in the main method; their separate pretraining variant costs 2M steps and is worse on harder navigation tasks. Requires two learners, intrinsic goal rewards and policy state. Our 50-step task is much shorter. Changing the evaluator to execute arbitrary hierarchy code would violate the current safe artifact contract; a future restricted schema would need independent review. |

## Predictive features and learning what to explore

| Paper and reading depth | Mechanism and evidence | Costs, limits and implication for AutoManip |
|---|---|---|
| **Schwarzer et al., SPR (ICLR 2021)** — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.05929). Read multi-step latent objective, Algorithm 1 and Appendix D. | Predicts future action-conditioned representations with an EMA target, augmenting Rainbow. Strong Atari100k evidence; the auxiliary objective need not reconstruct pixels. | 100k decisions correspond to 400k frames. Reported P100 runtime is about 4.6h with augmentation versus 2.1h for controlled Rainbow; no-augmentation values are 3.0h versus 1.4h. Those costs prohibit calling the improvement compute-free. For state manipulation, image augmentation gains do not transfer automatically; predictive features must improve decisions, not just prediction error. |
| **Sekar et al., Plan2Explore (ICML 2020)** — [paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/sekar20a/sekar20a.pdf). Read information-gain derivation, model/exploration loop and experimental setup. | Uses disagreement among five predictors to seek future information through a learned world model; later learns task behavior in imagination. Evaluated on visual DeepMind Control, with matched model sizes. | “Zero-shot” means no new task-specific interaction after an exploration/model-learning phase, not learning without experience. All exploration and imagined actor/model updates must count. Ensemble disagreement is a proxy for epistemic uncertainty, not a certificate. For our known single task, globally informative experience may be less useful than experience near grasp, release or stability boundaries. |

## Implications, not an algorithm shopping list

There are three different reasons an early learning curve can be flat: useful trajectories are rarely discovered; their targets carry poor credit; or optimization fails to retain an improvement. These predict different observations. More entropy addresses none of them universally. More replay helps only if its targets remain informative and its extra computation is affordable. Anchoring the actor can preserve behavior while preventing learning altogether.

Before selecting a large method, distinguish the direction of an estimated improvement from the size of its accepted update. Our existing BC-to-PPO failure offers a controlled starting behavior; it is assisted and cannot itself demonstrate from-scratch sample efficiency. A direction-versus-step-size probe can falsify an optimizer explanation before a full run. If it fails, the literature points toward target validity and exploration coverage rather than another learning-rate grid.

This review does not establish a universal optimizer defect, justify keeping PPO indefinitely, or rule out learned models. It identifies concrete comparisons and hidden costs to carry into the [scientific diagnosis and proposal](../research-strategy-diagnosis-20261028.md).
