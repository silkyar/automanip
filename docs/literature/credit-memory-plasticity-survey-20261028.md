# Credit, memory, plasticity and control: primary-source survey

Read on 2026-10-28. This is a literature assessment, not authorization to run anything. No infrastructure, training, evaluation, protected episode data or candidate code was accessed for this survey. “Prediction” below means our inference, not a finding established in StackCube.

The strongest recurring idea is to improve **what a stored transition teaches**, rather than merely repeat its current gradient. However, trajectory replay, adaptive representations, and stable optimization solve different problems. None of these papers establishes a tenfold interaction reduction for our state-based, short-horizon, contact-rich PPO task. Several reported improvements use substantially more learning work per interaction or privileged dynamics knowledge.

| Primary paper | Mechanism and evidence actually read | Interactions versus computation | AutoManip relevance, limits and novelty boundary |

| **Arjona-Medina et al., RUDDER (NeurIPS 2019)** — [paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2019/file/16105fb9cc614fc29e1bda00dab60d41-Paper.pdf) | Sequence-return prediction with an LSTM, then contribution-based reward redistribution; return-equivalence is the key theoretical constraint. Read §§2–4 and Atari results/Table 1. Artificial delayed-reward tasks give large gains; Atari learning curves favor RUDDER in 32/52 games, final scores in 29/52. | Synthetic “learning time” is episodes, not seconds. Adds predictor/replay/redistribution work. Atari baseline also includes exploration modifications described in the paper; do not compare its headline directly with vanilla PPO. | Relevant only if delayed causal credit survives existing GAE/dense shaping. Return prediction is not causal identification: later correlates can explain away earlier actions. A learned success reward without return correction would not reproduce RUDDER. |
| **Munos et al., Retrace(λ) (NeurIPS 2016)** — [paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2016/file/c3992e9a68c5ae12bd18488bc579b30d-Paper.pdf) | Off-policy multistep targets use trace coefficient `λ min(1,π/μ)`. Read operator/proof assumptions, trace trade-off and §5: DQN-style short-sequence replay on 60 Atari games. | Reuses interactions through sequence target computation. Behavior probabilities/densities must be known or estimated. Contraction/convergence results concern the stated operators/tabular conditions, not arbitrary neural actor–critic optimization. | Useful distinction between safe critic reuse and actor policy-gradient reuse. Clipping a PPO ratio does not turn stale PPO data into Retrace. Continuous-action trace densities are discussed, but expected-action Q computation and critic design still need a real adaptation. |
| **Lee, Choi & Chung, Episodic Backward Update (ICLR 2019; revised paper2019)** — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1805.12375) | Samples episodes; a temporary Q-table recursively propagates targets backward with a diffusion factor. Read §3, stochastic theorem conditions and §5. Mazes plus49 Atari games. Constant β=.5 beats Nature DQN in 33/49; adaptive variant in 39/49 at 10M frames. | The often-quoted5%/10% comparisons match aggregate mean/median Nature-DQN scores, not every task. **Adaptive EBU trains 11 parallel learners**; fixed-β Table 2 reports 152 versus 138 hours for DQN across 49 games. Adaptive timing uses multiple GPUs, so reported hours are not a matched GPU-hour budget. | Strong precedent for target construction, weak evidence for a PPO plug-in. Stochastic convergence requires restrictive β bounds; repeated maxima can overestimate. GAE already computes backward traces, so merely reversing minibatch order is not EBU or a new credit mechanism. |
| **Brittain et al., Prioritized Sequence Experience Replay (2019/2020 version)** — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.12726) | TD-error priority decays backward to preceding transitions; importance weighting and a priority-retention floor address bias/collapse. Read §3, theorem assumptions, §§4–6. DQN comparisons across 60 Atari games; reported aggregates use 55 with human scores. |200M frames/run; reported5–10 days on one GPU. Larger decay windows cost more. Table 1 uses the **best agent snapshot**, not only final performance. The faster-convergence theorem is for prefilled exhaustive Blind Cliffwalk replay, not general nonlinear RL. | Temporal prioritization is distinct from giving all successful episodes more weight. Prioritizing large TD errors can also prioritize noise. Importing it into PPO changes sampling and actor weighting; any benefit needs correction and a matched-compute comparator. |
| **Nikishin et al., The Primacy Bias in Deep RL (ICML 2022)** — [paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/nikishin22a/nikishin22a.pdf) | Periodically resets network parts while preserving replay. Read priming experiment, reset mechanism and replay-ratio/n-step ablations. Evidence spans Atari100k and DeepMind Control, including SAC. Heavy early fitting can impair later learning. | Reset operation itself is cheap; high replay ratios still consume more gradient work. Benefits are particularly large at high ratios. Preserving the replay buffer is central to relearning after resets. | Our on-policy PPO discards old rollouts, so resetting it lacks this recovery memory. A plateau alone does not establish primacy bias. This is established prior art for selective forgetting, not a novel neural “sleep” mechanism. |
| **Sokar et al., The Dormant Neuron Phenomenon / ReDo (ICML 2023)** — [paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/sokar23a/sokar23a.pdf) | Measures relative activation; periodically reinitializes dormant units' incoming weights and zeros outgoing weights. Read Algorithm1, §§4–5 and outgoing-weight ablation. DQN 17 games, DrQ 26 Atari100k games, SAC 4 MuJoCo tasks; multiple seeds and bootstrap intervals. | Higher replay ratios explicitly increase training time/cost. ReDo adds activity measurement/reinitialization. Small thresholds limit output disturbance; random outgoing weights perform worse in the reported ablation. | An activity diagnostic can motivate a targeted intervention, but a low-activity unit is not automatically useless. Evidence largely concerns value-based/off-policy systems; our tanh PPO actor and critic need separate diagnosis. Recycling is known prior art, not guaranteed retained behavior. |
| **Lyle et al., Understanding Plasticity in Neural Networks (ICML 2023)** — [paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/lyle23b/lyle23b.pdf) | Defines plasticity through ability to fit new challenge targets; studies curvature, gradient interference and optimization. Read definition/probe methodology, falsification discussion, §6 and appendix caveats. LayerNorm improves DDQN across 57 Atari games, 3 seeds/game. | Probes themselves require extra optimization; improved challenge-target fitting is not environment interaction efficiency. Normalization adds computation; no universal wall-time multiplier is established. | **Dormancy is not a sufficient explanation**: plasticity loss occurs without saturation. Authors explicitly cannot definitively attribute Atari gains to restored plasticity. Categorical outputs can reduce probe loss yet destabilize policy learning and require optimizer retuning. |
| **Kirkpatrick et al., EWC (PNAS 2017)** — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.00796) | Diagonal-Fisher quadratic anchoring protects parameters important to previous tasks. Read Bayesian approximation, MNIST/Atari sequential-task experiments and Fisher perturbation test. It preserves old skills better than unregularized sequential training. | Fisher estimation adds replay minibatches; retained parameter/Fisher statistics and penalty work are real costs. Atari protection starts only after 20M frames/game; hyperparameters are tuned. It does not equal ten separately trained experts. | Preservation and plasticity pull in opposite directions. EWC is primarily a multi-task retention method, not evidence for accelerating one task from scratch. Fisher “null” directions still affect performance in the paper: parameter importance estimates are imperfect. |
| **Dayan, The Successor Representation (Neural Computation 1993)** — [original paper](https://www.gatsby.ucl.ac.uk/~dayan/papers/sr93.pdf) | Learns future-state occupancy features for TD generalization. Read §§3–5 and maze/barrier figures. Nearby coordinates across a barrier should not share value features; learned predictive geometry helps a small navigation problem. | Representation learning/latent exploration must be counted; “unrewarded” samples are still interactions. Original state-based matrix representation is not computationally free in continuous high-dimensional states. | Predictive features might distinguish physically close manipulation states with different futures. But the original paper already warns that policy specialization can degrade the SR and hinder a new goal. This is predictive representation, not necessarily recurrent memory. |
| **Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman, The Hippocampus as a Predictive Map (Nature Neuroscience 2017)** — [author paper](https://gershmanlab.com/pubs/Stachenfeld17.pdf), [publication](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4650) | SR-based computational account of place/grid patterns and policy/topology effects. Read SR equations, reward-change argument and discussion of policy dependence. Evidence is model simulation and correspondence with neuroscience findings. | Cached occupancy makes some reward revaluation cheap after learning the map; learning the map and its basis is still a cost. This is not a robot sample-efficiency experiment. | Supports a hypothesis about predictive state representations, not a claim that a hippocampus-inspired network learns StackCube faster. Reward revaluation with unchanged dynamics/policy is easier than contact-transition or policy changes. Brain analogy alone is not causal algorithm evidence. |
| **Hussing et al., Dissecting Deep RL with High Update Ratios: Combatting Value Divergence (RLJ/RLC 2024)** — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.05996), [journal](https://rlj.org/papers/hussing2024dissecting) | Reexamines priming: exploding in-distribution Q estimates can explain prolonged failure; unit-ball output-feature normalization supports high-UTD SAC. Read §§3–5 and limitations. DeepMind Control includes difficult dog tasks. | High UTD buys sample reuse with more learning work. UTD 32 is not automatically better than 8; median/quantile results overlap and actor resets retain benefits. | Important negative evidence against diagnosing all failures as primacy/representation loss. This concerns off-policy Q bootstrapping; PPO's V/GAE mechanism differs. Reducing value divergence does not fix exploration or every actor failure. |
| **Sun, Jha & Fan, Learning Certified Control Using Contraction Metric (CoRL 2020)** — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.12569) | Joint neural controller/metric synthesis for control-affine dynamics. Read §§3–4 and validation caveats: nine simulated models with known dynamics or a learned model. | Offline states/dynamics derivatives replace RL interactions; count identification and verification. Reported CPU latency is **inference**, not learning time. | Promising for local trajectory stabilization, not demonstrated pick/grasp/release learning. Valid certificates require stated domain/regularity and verification conditions; sampled low loss alone is not a global guarantee. Hybrid contacts, saturation and missing reference trajectories obstruct direct transfer. |

Five cross-connections and falsifiable predictions

1. **Credit transport versus gradient repetition.** EBU and RUDDER suggest that reusing an episode helps when it changes the learning target or reward assignment; repeating a frozen GAE target only optimizes the existing estimate. Prediction: a genuine delayed-credit intervention should improve earlier-stage value/advantage accuracy on training episodes before changing final success. Falsifier: all gain disappears against an equally expensive longer-trace target, or only gradient count changes. First establish an actual delayed-credit bottleneck; short episodes and shaped rewards can make this literature irrelevant. [EBU](https://arxiv.org/html/1805.12375), [RUDDER](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2019/file/16105fb9cc614fc29e1bda00dab60d41-Paper.pdf).

2. **Replay needs both relevance and policy validity.** PSER determines which predecessors deserve learning; Retrace determines how stale trajectories can contribute safely to a target. Prediction: relevant recent sequences outperform an equal number of uniform replay rows only when effective trace lengths remain nontrivial. A stale success buffer can have strong labels but near-zero usable policy overlap. Negative control: matched priority with shuffled temporal adjacency; failure to beat it weakens a sequence-credit explanation. A critic-only replay result would not validate stale actor updates. [PSER](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.12726), [Retrace](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2016/file/c3992e9a68c5ae12bd18488bc579b30d-Paper.pdf).

3. **Preserve knowledge selectively, but distinguish rigidity from bad targets.** EWC protects important parameters; ReDo frees weakly used capacity; primacy resets discard fitted structure. These are competing interventions rather than ingredients to stack automatically. Prediction: if lost trainability is causal, later checkpoints fit a fixed new training-only challenge more poorly at matched updates, and a targeted intervention restores that ability. Falsifier: only Q scale/target errors explain recovery, or reset gains require more total optimization. Fisher/dormancy alone cannot identify the remedy. [EWC](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.00796), [ReDo](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/sokar23a/sokar23a.pdf), [plasticity](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/lyle23b/lyle23b.pdf), [divergence](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.05996).

4. **A predictive map should represent future outcomes, not just compress state.** SR and hippocampal models suggest learning distinctions between apparently similar states that lead to different futures. Prediction: predictive features help when hand/object/contact configurations with similar coordinates have distinct subsequent occupancy or success paths. Falsifier: a same-size generic auxiliary predictor gives equal benefit, or gains vanish after charging auxiliary data/updates. Policy dependence predicts negative transfer when later policies visit different transitions. This does not by itself justify recurrent memory in an already Markov state interface. [Dayan](https://www.gatsby.ucl.ac.uk/~dayan/papers/sr93.pdf), [predictive map](https://gershmanlab.com/pubs/Stachenfeld17.pdf).

5. **Local control stability and exploration are different objectives.** Contraction constrains convergence near a reference; plasticity preserves ability to adapt; neither creates a successful reference trajectory. Prediction: a valid local stabilizer mainly improves perturbation recovery after reaching an appropriate phase, rather than initial discovery. Falsifier: benefit comes only from supplying a planner/teacher whose data cost was excluded, or a contraction penalty reduces purposeful contact changes. A useful learned metric without verified conditions remains a regularizer, not a safety certificate. [C3M](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.12569), [plasticity](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/lyle23b/lyle23b.pdf).

Reading depth and decision limits

All 12 rows use original papers, not secondary summaries. Reading was targeted to the methods, stated assumptions, experimental setup, relevant ablations and limitations identified in each row; it was not an independent proof verification or code reproduction. EBU/PSER/EWC/C3M/high-UTD were read in full-text HTML; RUDDER/Retrace/primacy/ReDo/plasticity/Dayan/predictive-map through extracted paper PDFs. C3M used arXiv after an OpenReview browser block. EWC's PMC page was inaccessible; the original authors' arXiv full text was used. No claim depends solely on an abstract.

For any later proposal, keep interaction count, actor/critic optimizer calls, replay/auxiliary training rows, dynamics/teacher/prefix acquisition, wall time and initialization provenance separate. A demonstrated improvement against old Nature DQN, toy navigation, task retention, or model-known tracking cannot be translated into a 10× StackCube forecast. The survey supports choosing a measured bottleneck and one mechanism for review; it does not support launching a bundle or treating biological plausibility as validation.
