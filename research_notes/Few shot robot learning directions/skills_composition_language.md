# Skill Decomposition, Compositional Skill Reuse, and Language/LLM-Driven Task Decomposition for Robot Manipulation

Scope note: these notes target AutoManip, an autoresearch loop on ManiSkill that currently trains single-task state-based PPO from scratch and keeps failing at the "release" step. Numbers were checked against primary sources (arXiv, project pages, PMLR or ICLR proceedings) where possible. Items marked **[from memory, unverified]** come from my background knowledge of canonical papers. I did not re-check their numbers in this session, so the report writer should treat them as lower-confidence. Third-party summaries (liner.com, emergentmind, wizwand) are flagged where they were the only source.

## 1. Skill/option discovery, skill priors, and primitive libraries

### Takeaway
There are two families. Hand-specified parameterized primitive libraries (RAPS, MAPLE, DMPs) reliably speed up RL and transfer to real hardware on short-to-medium manipulation tasks. Unsupervised skill discovery (DIAYN-style) and learned skill priors (SPiRL) help mainly with exploration and need either large offline data or a lot of pretraining. For a small state-based ManiSkill loop, a small fixed library of parameterized primitives (reach, grasp, move, place, *release/open-gripper*) is the most practical option, and it is the most direct structural fix for a missing release step.

### Cited Findings
- **Options framework** (Sutton, Precup & Singh, 1999): this paper formalized temporally extended actions ("options": an initiation set, an intra-option policy, and a termination condition) within SMDPs. It is the theoretical basis for skill chaining and hierarchical RL. — [Sutton et al. 1999, AIJ](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0004370299000521) **[from memory, unverified link]**
- **DIAYN** (Eysenbach, Gupta, Ibarz, Levine, 2018/ICLR 2019): learns diverse skills without reward by maximizing the mutual information between skill and state. The skills are used as primitives for hierarchical RL and for fine-tuning. The evidence comes mostly from locomotion/MuJoCo control, not contact-rich manipulation. — [arXiv 1802.06070](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06070) **[from memory, unverified]**
- **SPiRL** (Pertsch, Lee, Lim, CoRL 2020): learns a latent skill embedding plus a state-conditioned *skill prior* from large unlabeled offline datasets. A downstream high-level RL policy is then regularized toward the prior. The authors report that it beats flat RL and prior-free skill-based RL on maze navigation and kitchen manipulation. — [arXiv 2010.11944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11944) **[from memory, unverified]**
- **RAPS** (Dalal, Pathak, Salakhutdinov, NeurIPS 2021): an RL agent outputs which parameterized primitive to run (lift, grasp, move-delta, open/close gripper, and others) along with its continuous arguments. It "significantly outperform[s] prior methods that learn skills from offline expert data", is agnostic to the RL algorithm, and gave a 32x speedup in execution on a real xArm 6 compared with raw actions (secondary summary). — [arXiv 2110.03655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03655); [NeurIPS 2021](https://nips.cc/virtual/2021/35758); [liner summary](https://liner.com/review/accelerating-robotic-reinforcement-learning-via-parameterized-action-primitives)
- **MAPLE** (Nasiriany, Liu, Zhu, ICRA 2022, Outstanding Learning Paper Award): adds a library of predefined behavior primitives (reach, grasp, push, and others) to standard RL, keeping atomic low-level actions as a fallback. It is evaluated on 8 tasks, 6 from robosuite plus cleanup and peg-insertion. It outperforms baselines "by a significant margin", and the stack and cleanup policies were executed on real hardware. — [MAPLE project page](https://ut-austin-rpl.github.io/maple)
- **Dynamic Movement Primitives** (Ijspeert, Nakanishi, Hoffmann, Pastor, Schaal, Neural Computation 2013): stable attractor-based trajectory primitives that can be learned from a single demonstration and generalized to new goals. They are the classical "one demo, one skill" building block. — [Ijspeert et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00393) **[from memory, unverified]**
- **Agentic Skill Discovery** (Zhao, Weber, Wermter; arXiv May 2024; *Robotics and Autonomous Systems* 2026): the authors argue that LLM planners need an initial skill library ("a 'grasping' capability can never emerge from a skill library containing only diverse 'pushing' skills"). In their method the LLM proposes tasks and writes reward and success functions, RL trains each skill, rewards are evolved, and a VLM verifies success. The library grows "from zero skill". The authors stress that false-positive success checks contaminate the library. — [arXiv 2405.15019](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15019v2); [project](https://agentic-skill-discovery.github.io)
- **Skill chaining failure mode, directly relevant to "stalls at release"**: adversarial skill chaining with terminal-state regularization (T-STAR; Lee, Lim, et al., CoRL 2021) targets the mismatch between where one skill ends and the states the next skill can start from. **[from memory, unverified; arXiv 2111.07999]**

### Inferences
- The AutoManip release stall looks like a classic *missing or poorly terminated sub-skill* problem. RAPS and MAPLE both make "open gripper" an explicit discrete primitive. Under a primitive action space, a policy cannot fail to release through a gradual exploration shortfall: release is one action choice.
- Unsupervised discovery (DIAYN) is unlikely to be a cheap win for state-based ManiSkill PickCube/Place. The evidence base is mostly locomotion, and it needs long pretraining.
- AGENTS.md fixes the action interface for v0, so a primitive action space would count as a *new interface version* (v1). It should not be something the agent can edit.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve exact RAPS/MAPLE per-task success numbers. I found no study of RAPS/MAPLE on ManiSkill specifically.
- I did not verify the SPiRL, DIAYN, or T-STAR numbers in this session.

## 2. LLM/VLM planners over skills and LLM-written rewards: evidence for few-shot acquisition of new tasks

### Takeaway
LLM planners (SayCan, Code as Policies, Inner Monologue, ProgPrompt, VoxPoser) compose *existing* skills and perception APIs into new instructions zero-shot. They do not create new motor skills, and they fail when a needed primitive is missing or unreliable. LLM-written rewards (Eureka, Text2Reward, Language to Rewards, CurricuLLM, Agentic Skill Discovery) are the line of work that does acquire genuinely new low-level skills from a sentence. The cost is a full RL run per candidate. Text2Reward was evaluated on ManiSkill2, including PickCube. Text2Reward and Eureka have been reproduced and extended across groups; most of the other headline numbers are single-paper claims.

### Cited Findings
**Planners over a fixed skill set**
- **SayCan** (Ahn et al., 2022): an LLM scores skills by usefulness for the instruction, and learned value functions score feasibility. On 101 real-kitchen instructions, PaLM-SayCan reached 84% planning success and 74% execution success. FLAN-SayCan reached 70% and 61%. It struggled with negations ("a snack that isn't an apple"). — [project](https://say-can.github.io); [arXiv 2204.01691](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.01691.pdf); [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2022/09/google-robot-saycan/)
- **Code as Policies** (Liang et al., 2022): an LLM writes Python policy code calling perception and control APIs, with hierarchical code generation. — [arXiv 2209.07753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07753) **[from memory; I did not retrieve its original success numbers]**. A 2025 reliability study (RoboInspector) tested Code-as-Policies-style generation across 48 task/instruction/LLM combinations. Success varied widely, from about 0.40–0.50 for weaker LLMs to about 0.90–0.94 for the best, and the study concludes that LLM policy code is unreliable. — [arXiv 2508.21378](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21378)
- **Inner Monologue** (Huang et al., 2022) adds closed-loop language feedback (success detectors, scene descriptions) to LLM planning. **ProgPrompt** (Singh et al., ICRA 2023) uses program-style prompts listing the available actions and objects. — [arXiv 2207.05608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05608), [arXiv 2209.11302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11302) **[from memory, numbers not verified]**
- **VoxPoser** (Huang et al., CoRL 2023): an LLM plus VLM compose 3D affordance/constraint value maps, which a model-based planner turns into zero-shot closed-loop trajectories for free-form language tasks in sim and on real robots. — [arXiv 2307.05973](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.05973.pdf)
- **Plan-Seq-Learn (PSL)** (Dalal, Chiruvolu, Chaplot, Salakhutdinov, ICLR 2024): an LLM produces a high-level plan, motion planning moves the robot to the region of each stage, and RL learns only the local contact-rich interaction. The abstract versions disagree: the ICLR proceedings report >80% success on 20+ tasks across 4 benchmarks from raw visual input, while arXiv v1 says >85%. Tasks have up to 10 stages. A secondary summary reports 95% vs 38% (TAMP) and 37% (SayCan) on multi-stage tasks; I could not verify this. — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/2e9f9cde1b709281a06dd14f679e4c51-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2405.01534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01534v1); [liner (unverified)](https://liner.com/review/planseqlearn-language-model-guided-rl-for-solving-long-horizon-robotics)
- **Voyager** (Wang et al., 2023; Minecraft, not robotics): the agent builds an ever-growing library of executable code skills, each indexed by an embedding of its description, alongside an automatic curriculum and iterative self-verification. It gets 3.3x more unique items, reaches tech-tree milestones up to 15.3x faster, and travels 2.3x longer distances than prior SOTA. — [arXiv 2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16291); [GitHub](https://www.github.com/MineDojo/Voyager)

**LLM-written rewards and curricula (new skills from language)**
- **Eureka** (Ma et al., ICLR 2024): GPT-4 reads raw environment source code plus a task description, writes reward code, and evolves it in-context using training statistics. Across 29 environments and 10 morphologies it beat expert human rewards on 83% of tasks, with a 52% average *normalized* improvement. Its rewards were weakly correlated with human ones, and less so on harder tasks. Performance drops with GPT-3.5. It enabled dexterous pen spinning. — [project](https://eureka-research.github.io); [ICLR 2024](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/18971); [arXiv 2310.12931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12931v2)
- **DrEureka** (Ma et al., RSS 2024) extends Eureka to sim-to-real by having the LLM also write domain-randomization configurations. — [arXiv 2406.01967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01967) **[from memory, unverified]**
- **Text2Reward** (Xie et al., ICLR 2024): generates *dense, shaped* reward code from a compact Pythonic environment abstraction. On 17 manipulation tasks (ManiSkill2 plus MetaWorld), its rewards matched or beat expert-written rewards on 13. On 4 tasks it beat the human oracle, either in convergence speed (ManiSkill2 OpenCabinetDoor) or in success (**ManiSkill2 PickCube**). Few-shot prompting beat zero-shot on 2 of the 6 hard ManiSkill2 tasks. The biggest error class in 100 generated rewards was semantic/logic misalignment. An ablation that removed the if-else stage structure from expert rewards failed on harder tasks, which is evidence that staged rewards matter. — [arXiv 2309.11489](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11489v3); [project](https://text-to-reward.github.io)
- **Language to Rewards** (Yu et al., CoRL 2023): an LLM writes reward parameters/code, and MuJoCo MPC optimizes actions in real time. It achieved about 90% reliability on 17 quadruped and dexterous-manipulator tasks. The baseline using primitive skills / Code-as-Policies reached about 50% (that figure comes from a garbled abstract copy, so verify it). It was validated on a real arm (pick, open drawer). — [arXiv 2306.08647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08647); [Google blog](https://research.google/blog/language-to-rewards-for-robotic-skill-synthesis/)
- **CurricuLLM** (Ryu et al., ICRA 2025): an LLM writes a sequence of natural-language subtasks, translates each into reward and goal-distribution code, and evaluates policies from rollouts. Prior subtask code is fed forward. It was tested on manipulation, navigation, and locomotion, with real humanoid validation. I did not retrieve its numbers. — [arXiv 2409.18382](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18382v2); [code](https://github.com/labicon/CurricuLLM)
- **RoboGen** (Wang et al., 2023) uses an LLM to propose tasks, generate scenes, decompose them into subtasks, and choose RL, motion planning, or trajectory optimization per subtask, all with LLM-written rewards. — [arXiv 2311.01455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01455) **[from memory, numbers not verified]**
- **GenSim** (Wang et al., ICLR 2024): an LLM synthesizes *simulation task code* in a growing task library (Ravens/CLIPort). Joint training on GPT-4-generated tasks improved generalization by 50%, with about 40% zero-shot transfer to new simulated tasks and about 25% better real-world generalization from sim pretraining. GenSim2 (2024) adds multimodal LLMs and RL/planner solvers. — [arXiv 2310.01361](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.01361); [project](https://gen-sim.github.io); [GenSim2](https://huggingface.co/papers/2410.03645)

### Inferences
- How strong is the evidence for "genuinely new tasks"? For planners, the skill set is closed: novelty lives in the *composition*, and success is capped by primitive reliability (SayCan's 84% planning vs 74% execution gap). For LLM rewards, new skills do appear (Eureka pen spinning, Text2Reward ManiSkill tasks). That is "few-shot in language" but not "few-shot in compute": each candidate still needs a full RL run.
- Replication status: the "LLM writes reward code that rivals humans" claim is reproduced across groups (Eureka, Text2Reward, L2R, CurricuLLM, Agentic Skill Discovery all report it independently). Specific percentages (83%, 13/17, 90%) are single-paper numbers on each paper's own task suites.
- Text2Reward's ablation and error analysis suggest a direct fix for the release stall: an LLM-written *staged* reward with an explicit "gripper open AND object at goal AND gripper away" stage. The stock ManiSkill dense reward may not shape this. AutoManip should check what the stock PickCube/Place dense reward actually encodes.
- This fits AutoManip's AGENTS.md principle "reward editable only through narrow interfaces": an Eureka/Text2Reward-style reward function *is* that narrow interface. The trusted evaluator should stay separate from the LLM-written reward and its success checks (Agentic Skill Discovery warns about false-positive success contamination).

### Gaps
- I found no independent replication that reports Eureka's exact 83% figure on a different task suite.
- I did not retrieve the original Code as Policies, Inner Monologue, ProgPrompt, RoboGen, or CurricuLLM numbers.
- 2025–2026 VLA-hierarchy successors (for example "Hi Robot"-style high-level VLM plus low-level VLA, and Gemini Robotics) were not searched in this session.

## 3. Segmenting a single demonstration into skills and mapping it onto a library

### Takeaway
Practical, replicated methods segment demos at **object-contact / gripper-state changes** or with **waypoint compression**, rather than with learned latent segmentation. MimicGen's object-centric subtask segmentation is the most proven route from "one or a few demos" to "many demos". Gripper open/close events are natural segment boundaries, which makes "release" an explicit, detectable segment.

### Cited Findings
- **CompILE** (Kipf et al., ICML 2019): learns latent segmentations and encodings of demonstrations jointly, with no labels, and reuses the segments as sub-policies. — [arXiv 1812.01483](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01483) **[from memory, unverified]**
- **MimicGen** (Mandlekar et al., CoRL 2023): from about 200 human demos it generated over 50K demos across 18 tasks. 200 demos generated from 10 human demos performed comparably to 200 human demos. Source demos are split into *object-centric subtask segments*, which are transformed to new object poses and stitched together. Sources disagree on whether boundaries are hand-annotated or heuristic (contact/gripper based), so check the paper. — [arXiv 2310.17596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17596); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/mandlekar23a.html). SkillMimicGen (Garrett et al., 2024) extends it with explicit skill segments plus motion planning between them. — [arXiv 2410.18907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18907) **[from memory, unverified]**
- **AWE: Automatic Waypoint Extraction** (Shi, Sharma, Zhao, Finn, CoRL 2023): compresses a demo into the fewest waypoints whose linear interpolation stays within an error budget. It is a BC preprocessing step for ACT and Diffusion Policy, needs no human labels, gives up to 25% higher success in sim and 4–28% on real bimanual tasks, and shortens the decision horizon by up to 10x. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/shi23b.html); [arXiv 2307.14326](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.14326v1.pdf)
- **BUDS** (Zhu et al., 2022) and **LOTUS** (Wan, Zhu, Shah, Zhu, ICRA 2024): unsupervised segmentation of unsegmented demos into a *growing* library of goal-conditioned visuomotor skills, with a meta-controller that picks skills by index. In LOTUS, each new task's segments either add a new skill or update an existing one. It was evaluated on LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Goal, and LIBERO-50, reporting >11% higher average success than SOTA continual-learning baselines (ER, BUDS, sequential fine-tuning). — [arXiv 2311.02058](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.02058v4); [code](https://github.com/UT-Austin-RPL/Lotus)
- **UVD: Universal Visual Decomposer** (Zhang et al., 2023): segments a video demo into subgoals with pretrained visual representations, with no task-specific training. — [arXiv 2310.08581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08581) **[from memory, unverified]**
- **ManiLong-Shot** (arXiv 2512.16302, Dec 2025): one-shot imitation for long-horizon manipulation. It decomposes the single demo into interaction-aware primitives and imitates each robustly. After training on 10 short-horizon tasks it generalizes to 20 unseen long-horizon tasks in sim, a 22.8% relative improvement over SOTA. — [arXiv 2512.16302](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16302v1)
- Classical probabilistic segmentation plus a library (ProbS; Lioutikov et al., Humanoids 2015) improves segmentation and the movement-primitive library together. The library then assembled a chair in an order not seen in the demos. — [TU Darmstadt PDF](https://www.ias.tu-darmstadt.de/uploads/Site/EditPublication/lioutikov_humanoids_2015.pdf)

### Inferences
- In state-based ManiSkill, segmentation is nearly free. Gripper-qpos transitions and object-contact flags (both available in privileged state) give grasp, transport, place, and release boundaries without learning. That makes MimicGen-style "1 demo, then N demos, then BC or demo-augmented PPO" cheap. Release becomes an explicit, separately evaluable segment.
- The 2025 one-shot results (ManiLong-Shot) are single-paper and simulation-only.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain MimicGen's per-task numbers or the exact segmentation procedure.
- I found no paper that evaluates "segment one demo, then retrieve the matching skills from a *learned RL* library" on ManiSkill.

## 4. Task-and-motion planning hybrids (PDDLStream, learning-for-TAMP, LLM+TAMP)

### Takeaway
TAMP gives guaranteed composition from symbolic operators: pick, place, and release are explicit operators with preconditions and effects. Its costs are hand-written operators and samplers. LLM+TAMP work shows LLMs can stand in for the symbolic planner or for goal specification, but geometric feasibility checking is what makes long-horizon plans actually succeed (Text2Motion: 82% vs 13%).

### Cited Findings
- **PDDLStream** (Garrett, Lozano-Pérez, Kaelbling, ICAPS 2020): extends PDDL with "streams", conditional samplers for grasps, poses, and motions, so classical planners can handle continuous TAMP. — [arXiv 1802.08705](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08705) **[from memory, unverified]**
- **Text2Motion** (Lin, Agia, Migimatsu, Pavone, Bohg, Autonomous Robots 2023): an LLM proposes skill sequences, and learned Q-function feasibility heuristics plus geometric feasibility planning across the *whole* sequence resolve inter-skill geometric dependencies. Success was 82% (arXiv v3/v5; v1 said 64%) vs 13% for prior language planners. A secondary source says myopic baselines finished 50–80% of subgoals but rarely the full task. — [arXiv 2303.12153](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.12153v5)
- **LLM+P** (Liu et al., 2023): an LLM translates natural language into PDDL, and a classical planner solves it. This outperforms LLM-only planning on PDDL domains. — [arXiv 2304.11477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11477) **[from memory, unverified]**
- PSL (section 2) is a learning-for-TAMP hybrid: a symbolic/LLM plan, a motion planner for free-space motion, and RL for contact. Its reported baseline numbers include TAMP (unverified secondary figure: 38%).
- **ReKep** (Huang et al., CoRL 2024): a VLM writes keypoint-based relational constraints for each stage, including release-stage constraints, and a hierarchical optimizer solves them. **[from memory, unverified; arXiv 2409.01652]**

### Inferences
- The Text2Motion result (myopic chaining completes subgoals but not full tasks) mirrors AutoManip's "grasp/transport/place OK, release fails" pattern. Downstream preconditions, such as whether the object is stable when released, have to be checked while the plan is being made, not after.
- The cheapest TAMP-flavored step for ManiSkill is to use ManiSkill's built-in motion-planning solutions (mplib-based) as scripted primitives. This gives an upper-bound oracle composition baseline. **[ManiSkill motion-planning solutions: from memory; verify against the pinned ManiSkill version]**

### Gaps
- I did not verify PDDLStream or LLM+P numbers, or AutoTAMP and other 2024–2026 LLM+TAMP successors.

## 5. How compositional generalization is evaluated (benchmarks and reported numbers)

### Takeaway
Benchmarks probe compositional and held-out generalization in different ways. CALVIN ABC→D tests chained instructions in an unseen environment. LIBERO tests spatial, object, goal, and long-horizon suites plus lifelong transfer. Meta-World ML10/ML45 tests held-out task adaptation. Meta-World meta-RL numbers stay low (ML45 meta-test ≤40%), while modern VLAs saturate LIBERO (>90% on some suites). Truly held-out *compositions* remain much harder than in-distribution tasks.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta-World** (Yu et al., CoRL 2019): ML45 trains on 45 tasks and tests on 5 held-out tasks. Meta-test success over 10 seeds was MAML 39.9%, RL² 33.3%, PEARL 22%. Meta-train success was MAML 40.7%, RL² 70%, PEARL 14.5%. On ML10 meta-test: MAML 31.6%, RL² 35.8%, PEARL 13%. The extracted table was garbled, so the column alignment is inferred. — [arXiv 1910.10897](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.10897); [eval docs](https://metaworld.farama.org/evaluation/evaluation/)
- **CALVIN** (Mees et al., 2022): the metric is average length of successfully completed chains of 5 language instructions (0–5), with the ABC→D unseen-environment split. Aggregator snapshots, all unverified: about 4.00 average length with 60.6% 5-step success for a 2025 method without external pretraining; OpenVLA 2.55 on ABC→D; ABCD→D top around 4.67. Some leaderboards use a non-comparable 10-task protocol (VAMPO 6.73, π0 3.61). — [CALVIN arXiv 2112.03227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03227) **[link from memory]**; [wizwand ABC→D](https://www.wizwand.com/sota/long-horizon-robot-manipulation-on-calvin-abc-d-long-horizon); [emergentmind](https://api.emergentmind.com/topics/calvin-dataset)
- **LIBERO** (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B): suites are Spatial, Object, Goal, Long (LIBERO-10), and LIBERO-90. On LIBERO-Long, OpenVLA scores 53.7%. π0 is reported inconsistently (73 vs 85 across papers). OmniVLA-RL (2026) reports 93.5%. On LIBERO-90, OpenVLA scores 62.0%, SmolVLA 68.9%, and NanoVLA-L 83.3%. — [LIBERO arXiv 2306.03310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03310) **[link from memory]**; [NanoVLA 2510.25122](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.25122); [OmniVLA-RL 2604.17706](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17706)
- **RLBench** (James et al., 2020) has 100 hand-designed tasks with demos and is commonly used for multi-task and few-shot evaluation. **ManiSkill** (ManiSkill2/3) has diverse tasks with dense rewards, which is the basis of Text2Reward's "expert reward" comparison. — [RLBench arXiv 1909.12271](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12271) **[from memory]**; [Text2Reward](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11489v3)
- GenSim's protocol trains on LLM-generated tasks and tests zero-shot on held-out human-designed tasks (about 40% zero-shot transfer). It is one of the few direct "new task without demos" measurements. — [GenSim](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.01361)

### Inferences
- High LIBERO numbers mostly reflect in-distribution multi-task imitation, not composition. Meta-World ML45 meta-test numbers (about 20–40%) and GenSim's about 40% zero-shot transfer give a more honest picture of held-out task acquisition.
- AutoManip could define a minimal compositional split using stock ManiSkill tasks: train on PickCube and PushCube, test on StackCube, PlaceSphere, or PullCube. The split should be frozen in the trusted evaluator and scored separately with stock success and strict place-and-release success, consistent with AGENTS.md. **[task names from memory; verify against the pinned ManiSkill version]**

### Gaps
- Leaderboard numbers for CALVIN and LIBERO come from third-party aggregators and inconsistent protocols. I found no standardized "held-out composition" benchmark on ManiSkill3.

## 6. Cheapest first experiment for the AutoManip autoresearch loop

### Takeaway
The cheapest and best-supported first experiment is an **Eureka/Text2Reward-style staged-reward experiment** on the existing PickCube/place task and then one held-out ManiSkill task. The LLM may write only the reward (and optionally curriculum) function behind a narrow interface. Each candidate is trained with the frozen PPO setup and scored by the trusted evaluator on strict place-and-release success. Text2Reward already showed that LLM rewards can beat the expert reward on ManiSkill2 PickCube. Second-cheapest is gripper-event segmentation of one scripted or motion-planned demo into grasp/transport/place/release segments, then demo-augmented PPO or a MimicGen-style replay.

### Cited Findings
- Text2Reward produced dense reward code on ManiSkill2 tasks that matched or beat expert rewards on 13 of 17 tasks and beat the oracle on PickCube success. Staged if-else rewards were what made hard tasks work. — [Text2Reward](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11489v3)
- Eureka's loop (sample reward candidates, train, feed back training statistics, refine) outperformed human rewards on 83% of 29 tasks. — [Eureka](https://eureka-research.github.io)
- CurricuLLM and Agentic Skill Discovery show the curriculum/library extension: subtask sequences with per-subtask reward code, and a skill library grown from zero with LLM-proposed tasks. They warn that unreliable success detection contaminates the library. — [CurricuLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18382v2); [ASD](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15019v2)
- PSL shows "LLM plan + motion planner + RL only for local contact" reaches >80% on multi-stage tasks. This is the natural second step once a few skills exist. — [PSL](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/2e9f9cde1b709281a06dd14f679e4c51-Abstract-Conference.html)
- MimicGen shows that 10 source demos, once segmented and replayed, can match 200 human demos. — [MimicGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17596)

### Inferences
- **Proposed experiment E1 (reward-only, CPU-reviewable, GPU-run later):** expose `reward_fn(obs_state, info) -> float` and an optional stage schedule as the *only* agent-editable surface. Give the LLM the task sentence plus the ManiSkill env state schema. Train with the frozen PPO config and a fixed compute budget, using paired dev seeds. Evaluate on strict release success with independent confirmation seeds. Compare against (a) the stock ManiSkill dense reward and (b) a hand-written staged reward that includes an explicit release stage. Log env steps and seeds.
