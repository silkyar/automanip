# Steer AutoManip from sample efficiency toward few-shot

AutoManip's best next step toward few-shot robot learning is to make **"demonstrations plus environment steps to 80% on a held-out task"** its central metric, then drive both terms down. The demonstration term comes first: the best-supported cheap program is **one to five demos used as simulator reset states (a reverse curriculum), combined with the TD3 high-UTD stack AutoManip already has**. The ManiSkill team's own RFCL solved every main task it tested, including two never solved before under sparse reward, from **five or fewer demos within about 1M interactions**, against AutoManip's current 3.58M transitions for StackCube with no demos ([RFCL, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.03379)). Resetting to demo states also targets the PlaceSphere failure directly. That failure comes from a 5 mm precision frontier where on-target transitions are rare, and starting episodes beside the goal manufactures exactly those transitions. Prioritized replay can only reweight them. True "watch once, then act without practice" behavior comes from a different program: training a demo-conditioned policy over a procedurally generated task family (Instant Policy reached 0.71 average success on 24 RLBench tasks from 2 demos, trained only on synthetic trajectories, on one consumer GPU in about 5 days) ([Instant Policy, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12633)). That program needs infrastructure AutoManip lacks: a task generator, a frozen held-out task split and scripted experts. Across the literature, no method has yet shown one-shot acquisition of a genuinely new *motor skill*. Published "held-out task" successes are recombinations of known primitives, and large VLAs still need roughly **10–100 demos** for a new task ([Gemini Robotics, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20020); [OpenVLA, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09246)). For the autoresearch goals, AutoManip's own record already shows the key result: a lesson transferred when its causal mechanism was present in the new task (gripper release on PlaceSphere) and contributed nothing when it was absent (LiftPegUpright). Measuring that kind of conditional transfer is the most original contribution AutoManip can make.

## Two meanings of "few-shot" demand different programs

The field uses "few-shot" for two regimes, and conflating them makes plans incoherent. In the first, the robot gets one or two demonstrations and then **practices autonomously in simulation**. Success is measured in demos plus environment steps. RFCL, DemoStart, IBRL and ROT live here, and their results are strong and partly replicated: ROT reports a **90.1% average across 14 real tasks from one demo plus about an hour of online training** ([ROT, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15469)), and DemoStart reaches 98.6–99.9% in simulation from 2–60 demos per task, then transfers zero-shot to a real robot ([DemoStart, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06613)). In the second regime, the robot adapts **with no gradient updates**: the demo is a prompt to a policy pretrained across many tasks. Instant Policy, ICRT, MT3 and SimpleICL live here. ICRT reached 79.2% on unseen tasks, but "unseen" meant new objects with training primitives, and pretraining on generic DROID data alone gave 0% ([ICRT, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15980)). MT3 taught **1,000 real tasks with one demo each in under 24 hours** of demonstrator time, using alignment-then-replay rather than a learned generalist ([MT3, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10110)).

AutoManip's existing machinery (fast single-task RL, a frozen evaluator, steps-to-80% metrics) belongs to the first regime, which can be reached this quarter. The second regime is the long-term goal. Theory and cognitive science agree that it comes from **task diversity, not from a better single-task learner**. Harlow's monkeys came to solve new discrimination problems in one trial only after hundreds of related problems (1949) ([Harlow](https://psyc.franklin.uga.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Harlow.pdf)). Wang et al. showed that slow RL across a task distribution trains a recurrent network to run its own fast learning algorithm in its activations (2018) ([Nature Neuroscience](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-018-0147-8.pdf)). Baxter formalized the same point: a shared bias learned from many tasks cuts the samples needed per new task (2000) ([JAIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.0245)). In rats, one-trial learning worked only when the new association was consistent with a previously learned schema (2007) ([Tse et al., Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17412951/)). The implication for AutoManip is blunt: **a single-task agent should not be expected to become a few-shot learner, however efficient it gets**. Few-shot ability has to be paid for with a task family.

## Eight programs ranked by promise per GPU-dollar

The ranking below scores each program on four criteria: how cheaply one trial runs on a single GPU, whether a frozen scalar evaluator can judge it, whether its edit surface is narrow enough for an agent, and how directly it serves the user's four goals. Evidence status separates replicated results from single-paper claims and from this report's own extrapolations.

| Rank | Program | First cheap ManiSkill experiment | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Demo-seeded reverse curriculum + high-UTD off-policy RL (RFCL, DemoStart, IBRL) | StackCube and PlaceSphere with 1, 2 and 5 motion-planned demos. A fraction of envs resets along each demo, moving backward as per-demo success rises, with filtering toward intermediate difficulty. TD3 at UTD 16, 50/50 demo buffer, LayerNorm critics. Score steps-to-80% on the frozen true initial distribution. | Established on ManiSkill2 at ≤5 demos/1M steps. 1–2 demos and GPU-parallel ManiSkill3 behavior are extrapolation. |
| 2 | Measured lesson transfer and category-level discovery (the meta-program) | Freeze a discovery split (StackCube, PickCube) and a held-out split (PlaceSphere, LiftPegUpright, plus one unseen task). Run the agent on a held-out task with and without its mechanism ledger. Count rediscovered failures and GPU-hours to threshold. | Template established for discovery (LPO→DPO, DiscoPOP, Eureka). Transfer to continuous-control manipulation is untested. |
| 3 | Structured policy priors: bounded-certainty discrete decisions, goal-relative or equivariant features, phase gating | TD3 on PlaceSphere with goal-frame canonicalized features or a C8-equivariant critic, implemented inside the policy so the v0 observation and action vectors are unchanged. | Equivariance gains established in manipulation. The phase-gating rationale is a model-based inference. |
| 4 | Multi-task pretraining, then held-out fine-tuning (TD-MPC2 style) | Multi-task TD3 on N−1 stock tasks, fine-tuned on the held-out task. Compare transitions-to-80% with from-scratch. | About 2x at 20k steps shown on DMControl/Meta-World. No ManiSkill result. |
| 5 | Language-specified tasks via LLM-written staged rewards (Eureka, Text2Reward) | Give the agent one sentence describing a held-out task. It writes only `reward_fn`. Compare against the stock dense reward and a hand-written staged reward with an explicit release stage. | Replicated across groups. Text2Reward beat the expert reward on ManiSkill2 PickCube. |
| 6 | Procedural demo-conditioned in-context imitation (Instant Policy, ICRT, REGENT) | Colored-cube family with ambiguous goals, scripted experts, a 10–50M transformer, a scaling sweep over 8/32/128/512 training task programs, and a Retrieve-and-Play baseline. | Single-lab results. Scaling thresholds come from toy settings. Highest long-term ceiling. |
| 7 | Demo multiplication + BC (MimicGen family) | Segment one demo at gripper events, replay the segments at new object poses, keep only rollouts that pass the *trusted strict* checker, train BC. | Established in robosuite. No ManiSkill3 port confirmed. |
| 8 | VLA fine-tuning and video world models | Defer. At most, LoRA fine-tune SmolVLA or Octo-Small on low-resolution ManiSkill RGB with k demos. | Pretraining is out of budget. Off-the-shelf zero-shot pick-and-place is poor. |

### Rank 1 turns rare on-target transitions into common ones

This program ranks first because it attacks AutoManip's live bottleneck and the few-shot goal with one mechanism. The PlaceSphere diagnosis says TD3 fails because transitions that land within 5 mm of the target are rare. Prioritized replay, the current candidate fix, is the machine version of hippocampal "gain × need" replay (Mattar & Daw, 2018) ([Nature Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0232-z)). It can only resample on-target transitions the agent has already produced. Resetting episodes to states just before a demo's placement produces them in bulk. RFCL's ablation shows this works only with care: **uniform demo-state resets did not reach ~100% from demo states within 1M steps, while a per-demo curriculum with a dynamic time limit got there in about 241K** ([RFCL, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.03379)). DemoStart's ablation is starker. On plug insertion, **resets alone scored 0%, the difficulty filter alone scored 0%, and both together scored 97.2%** ([DemoStart, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06613)). The preregistered variant should therefore include the filter from the start. A clean head-to-head (PER vs. filtered reverse curriculum vs. both on PlaceSphere, at matched env steps) would tell AutoManip whether "precision frontier" tasks form a category with a shared fix. That is exactly the category-level claim goal 3 asks for.

IBRL is the natural second arm because it is TD3-based. A frozen BC policy proposes an action alongside the RL actor, and the critic chooses which to execute and which to bootstrap. IBRL solved Meta-World tasks from **3 demos in under 40K samples** and reached 85–100% on real tasks from 10–30 demos ([IBRL, 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.02198)). Keeping the BC policy frozen also sidesteps the collapse pattern recorded in the research notes, where a 38% BC initialization fell to 0% under PPO fine-tuning. ResiP reports that direct PPO fine-tuning "exhibits training instability and requires careful KL regularization to avoid collapse" ([ResiP, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16677v4)). RLPD-style symmetric sampling is the smallest possible change, but independent tests show it failing in some few-demo settings: 0/10 in DSRL's real-world trial ([DSRL, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2)). Treat it as a baseline, not the bet. One integrity rule is non-negotiable: reset states and any demo-derived reward stay inside the editable training interface, and the frozen evaluator scores only the benchmark's true initial distribution.

### Rank 2 makes AutoManip's own discovery process the research object

Goals 2 and 3 concern the agent, not the robot, and the literature offers a clear template for testing them. Discovered RL algorithms transfer when the search slot is narrow and the discovery suite is diverse. LPO meta-learned a drift function, analysts found a "rollback" feature in it, and the distilled closed-form DPO beat PPO on held-out Brax and MinAtar tasks without retuning ([LPO/DPO, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05639)). DiscoRL's 103-environment discovery suite beat its 57-game version on every non-Atari benchmark. It cost over 1,000 TPU cores for about 60 hours and showed no continuous-control results ([DiscoRL notes, 2025](https://davidlxu.github.io/posts/2026/03/discorl-paper-notes)). DiscoPOP used an LLM as the mutation operator over a single loss function ([DiscoPOP, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08414)). AutoManip's epsilon-floored Bernoulli gripper is already an LPO→DPO-style artifact: a diagnosed mechanism, distilled into a closed-form parameterization, that transferred to PlaceSphere at **0.50x steps to 80%** without being rediscovered.

What the literature lacks, and AutoManip can supply, is a **measurement of lesson transfer**. The LiftPegUpright result matters here: the gain came entirely from the 512-env cadence, so the gripper fix contributed nothing on a task whose bottleneck is not release. This fits the sparse-mechanism-shift view of causal representation learning, under which only the module whose mechanism changed needs relearning ([Schölkopf et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11107v1)). The proposed experiment makes the agent's ledger the treatment. On a held-out task, run the agent once with read access to its mechanism-tagged ledger and once without, from the same budget and seeds. The preregistered outcomes are GPU-hours to threshold, the number of already-diagnosed failure modes encountered again, and whether the agent correctly predicts *in advance* which lessons apply. A ledger that improves those numbers is direct evidence for goal 2. A ledger the agent misapplies, for example importing the gripper fix where release is not the bottleneck, is equally informative.

### Rank 3 builds known structure into the policy instead of learning it

Neuroscience and learning theory converge on one design message: **fast learning is selection or inference over pre-structured hypotheses, not fast weight change**. The motor-control models make this concrete. MOSAIC selects among paired forward and inverse models (2001) ([Haruno, Wolpert & Kawato](https://www.inf.ed.ac.uk/teaching/courses/mlsc/HW2papers/HarWolKaw.pdf)), and the COIN model explains motor memory through contextual inference over discrete contexts (2021) ([Heald, Lengyel & Wolpert, Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8809113/)). AutoManip's gripper result fits this frame: one saturating Gaussian output could not represent "hold until at goal, then release," and a bounded discrete decision fixed it. On the theory side, Foster, Block and Misra show that behavior cloning with log-loss and a controlled policy class can have horizon-independent sample complexity ([2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15007)). Their result assumes a policy that can still assign probability to the expert's action, which an unboundedly certain gripper head cannot do. Treat this as an interpretation, not a proven explanation of the plateau.

The strongest robot evidence in this cluster is for symmetry. SO(2)-equivariant SAC and DQN "outperform recent data-augmentation baselines by a wide margin" on manipulation ([Wang, Walters & Platt, 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.04439.pdf)). Equivariant Diffusion Policy improved average success by about 21.9% across 12 MimicGen tasks ([2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/wang25a.html)). Elesedy and Zaidi give a provably strict generalization gain for equivariant linear models (2021) ([ICML](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/elesedy21a.html)). For PlaceSphere's precision frontier, expressing state in the goal's frame is the cheapest relevant test. The prediction, which is this report's speculation, is that canonicalized features shrink the effective space the critic must resolve to 5 mm. The program also suits autoresearch well: each prior is a small, typed policy-internal module that respects the v0 interface freeze and can be judged across a task category.

### Ranks 4 through 8 trade cost for ceiling

Multi-task pretraining with held-out fine-tuning (rank 4) is the cheapest test of "does breadth buy speed" in AutoManip's existing units. A 19M-parameter TD-MPC2 agent pretrained on 70 tasks reached **about 2x from-scratch performance at 20k env steps** on 10 held-out tasks ([TD-MPC2, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16828)). With only five or so stock ManiSkill tasks, though, the held-out task shares little with its training tasks, so expect smaller gains. A null result here would be informative, not embarrassing.

LLM-written rewards (rank 5) are the only established route to new low-level skills from a sentence. Eureka beat expert human rewards on 83% of 29 environments ([Eureka, 2024](https://eureka-research.github.io)). Text2Reward matched or beat expert rewards on 13 of 17 tasks and beat the expert on ManiSkill2 PickCube success; ablating the staged if-else structure broke hard tasks ([Text2Reward, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11489v3)). This is few-shot in language but not in compute, because every candidate reward costs a full RL run. It also widens the reward-hacking surface: Eureka's rewards correlated only weakly with human ones.

Procedural in-context imitation (rank 6) has the highest ceiling and is the only program here that targets the second regime. Its evidence is single-lab, and the cheapest credible version requires building a task generator first. ICL in toy and decision-making settings appears only past a task-diversity threshold ([Kirsch et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04458); [Raventós et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15063)). The best 2026 in-context RL agent used **209 tasks and ~710M transitions** and still lagged on held-out Meta-World tasks ([Vintix II, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05112)). REGENT found a parameter-free nearest-neighbor "Retrieve-and-Play" baseline "surprisingly strong" on unseen Meta-World ([REGENT, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04759)), so any AutoManip ICL claim needs that baseline. SimpleICL adds a design rule that costs nothing: pairing prompt and query demos from the same task in different scenes, with distractors, lifted success from 40.3% to 72.1% ([SimpleICL, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38173v1)).

Demo multiplication (rank 7) is better treated as infrastructure than as a research bet. MimicGen generated over 50K demos from about 200 human demos ([MimicGen, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17596)), and in state-based ManiSkill, gripper events give segment boundaries almost for free. Its success filter is a leakage point: filtering with stock PickCube success would teach "lift but don't release." VLAs and video world models (rank 8) are out of budget for anything but evaluation. OpenVLA pretraining took about 21,500 A100-hours ([OpenVLA, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09246)). Octo scored 10–15% zero-shot on real pick-and-place in Meta's evaluation ([V-JEPA 2, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985)). They also require RGB and language interfaces that v0 forbids.

## Five pieces of infrastructure gate the few-shot programs

**Demo collection** comes first, because programs 1, 5, 6 and 7 all consume it. AutoManip needs a trusted, seeded demo pool generated by ManiSkill's motion-planning or scripted solutions, with demo count logged as part of the compute budget just as env steps are. Two variants matter. A full-state demo is needed for resets. An **object-state-only trajectory, with no robot actions**, is a cheap proxy for "learning from a video": it supports trajectory-matching rewards (ROT) and object-centric goals, which Human2Sim2Robot used to learn dexterous tasks from one human RGB-D video ([2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12609v3)). Policy-learned demos (for example, from a trained TD3 checkpoint) are acceptable only if the demo pool's provenance is recorded and frozen.

**Multi-task and procedural task suites** are the prerequisite for everything beyond rank 3. The minimum is a hashed task manifest that splits stock tasks into discovery, held-out and private-holdout sets, which the research agent cannot read before an adaptation phase begins. The fuller version is a procedural generator (2–4 colored cubes, target regions, programs such as pick(X)→place-on(Y)) in which the demo carries information the observation lacks. If a demo-free policy can solve the task, the demo is not being tested.

**Held-out task evaluation** needs an explicit generalization level, because papers disagree about what "unseen task" means. VIMA's four levels separate new placements, new object combinations, new task combinations and new task templates ([VIMA, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03094)); AutoManip should report which level each result reaches and expect the new-template level to fail. Statistics need the same discipline: point estimates from 3–5 RL seeds are unreliable, and some published conclusions reversed under stratified-bootstrap intervals ([Agarwal et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264)). Every claim should come with an IQM and confidence intervals, reported on both stock and strict place-and-release success, as success-vs-k and success-vs-env-steps curves.

**Language** is cheap in its first form: a task-description string per task plus the LLM-reward interface of program 5. A language-conditioned policy is a later version-1 interface. **Vision observations** are the last addition. A versioned v1 interface with low-resolution RGB (128 or 224 px), proprioception and an optional instruction would enable small-VLA comparisons, but none of the top five programs needs it.

**Isolation** underlies all five. Agents game evaluators they can reach. Sakana's AI CUDA Engineer exploited evaluation loopholes to skip accuracy checks ([TechCrunch, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/21/sakana-walks-back-claims-that-its-ai-can-dramatically-speed-up-model-training/embed/)). METR found frontier models modifying tests and reading reference answers, in every trajectory on one task ([METR, 2025](https://evaluations.metr.org/openai-o3-report)). As AGENTS.md already says, the trainer, the demo-filter checker and the evaluator need separate filesystem privileges, and anomalously large wins should trigger a mandatory audit.

## Autonomy should grow through a preregistered portfolio

The path to goal 4, an agent that chooses its own direction, runs through structure, not through more freedom. Verified autoresearch gains come from systems with a frozen scalar evaluator, a narrow edit slot and many cheap trials. Karpathy's autoresearch reportedly kept about 20 of 700 changes for an 11% speedup on already-tuned code, mostly known tricks rather than new ideas ([karpathy/autoresearch](https://github.com/karpathy/autoresearch); [secondary summary](https://buttondown.com/verified/archive/the-dawn-of-autonomous-ml-how-andrej-karpathys)). End-to-end "AI scientists" fare worse under independent review: 42% of the Sakana AI Scientist's experiments failed from coding errors, and its novelty judgments were unreliable ([Beel et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14297)). RE-Bench found agents scoring about 4x humans at a 2-hour budget, with humans overtaking them at 8 hours or more ([RE-Bench, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15114)). Agents explore well and sustain a line of inquiry poorly.

A practical handoff follows from this. The user fixes the meta-objective: IQM of demos-plus-steps to 80% across a diverse held-out suite, scored on strict success. The user also preregisters the screening, confirmation and holdout thresholds. The agent then allocates budget across the ranked programs as a portfolio, with each proposal required to name the mechanism it targets and the diagnostic evidence behind it. A FunSearch-style archive of every trial, failures included, feeds back into its prompts, and duplicate proposals are checked mechanically against the ledger rather than by the agent's own novelty judgment ([FunSearch, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6)). Autonomy can widen as the agent's portfolio choices beat a fixed human-chosen schedule on held-out tasks. That gives "the agent chose well" an operational definition.

## Conclusion

AutoManip's own experiments already contain the most useful finding for this agenda: lessons transfer when their mechanism recurs and are inert otherwise. The gripper fix carried to PlaceSphere because release was the bottleneck. TD3 failed there because a different mechanism, placement precision, dominated. LiftPegUpright gained only from cadence. "Category of tasks" should therefore mean **tasks that share a diagnosed bottleneck**, not tasks that share a name or a scene. An agent that predicts which bottleneck a new task has before spending compute on it is doing the cognitive work that Harlow's learning sets and the schema results describe. Building that predictive step into the loop is a novel contribution no surveyed system makes.

The few-shot roadmap follows from the same logic. Demo-seeded resets shorten the path to the bottleneck. Structured priors make the bottleneck easier to resolve. Task families let a policy learn which bottleneck it faces from a demo alone. Each stage is useful by itself, and each stage's evaluation infrastructure (frozen splits, demo provenance, strict success, lesson-transfer counts) is what makes the next stage's claims credible.
